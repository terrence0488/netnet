# P3-T20: Insights — the average's percent agrees with its amount

Status: todo
Depends on: none
Parallel-safe: yes

## Goal

Insights' summary line states one average: the mean change over the periods shown, and the same
change as a share of where they started. The percent never rises while the amount falls.

## Context

- Test report issue 2: monthly changes of −$4,000 (−44.4%), +$1,300 (+26%), +$1,000 (+15.9%), $0
  and +$1,000 (+13.7%) read "Averaging −$140 (+2.2%) a month over the last 5 months". Net worth
  fell from $9,000 to $8,300 (−7.8%). `summarisePeriods` (`packages/core/src/period-summary.ts`)
  takes the mean change, which is right, and the mean of the periods' percents, which can
  disagree with it in sign. The prototype (`docs/02-prototype/app.js`, `renderInsights`) averages
  percents the same way, and this task corrects it. PRD J11 and the architecture do not define
  the average. The rule is in `decisions.md` (Holdings, accessibility and packs).
- **The percent:** the mean change as a share of the opening total, `avgPct = (sum of changes ÷
  count) ÷ |opening| × 100`, worked from the exact sum, not from the rounded `avgAbs`. The opening
  total is the end value of the period before the first averaged one, which is `first.endValue −
  first.change`. Its sign is always the amount's, and it uses the same `|previous|` as each
  period's `changePct` (`packages/core/src/periods.ts`). An opening of 0 has no percent. In the
  example: −$140 on $9,000 is −1.6%.
- **The periods averaged:** every period in the set given whose change is not null. Since P3-T16
  the series' first period has a null change, so `summarisePeriods`' `index > 0` filter now only
  drops a ranged view's oldest period (6M, YTD, …), although that period has an earlier one to
  compare with. The prototype counts it. P3-T16's report noted the gap. "Over the last N months"
  counts the periods averaged.
- `get_period_changes` returns no average, so the agent's side is unchanged.

## Interface contract

- `PeriodSummary` (`@netnet/core`): `count` is the number of periods given whose change is not
  null. `avgAbs` is unchanged: the mean change in minor units, half away from zero. `avgPct:
  number | null` is as above, and null when the opening total is 0. `summarisePeriods` is null
  when no period has a change, as now.
- Insights' summary line: "Averaging −$140 (−1.6%) a month over the last 5 months". With no
  percent: "Averaging +$500 a month over the last 2 months". The other summary lines (filtered
  out, nothing tracked, not enough history) are unchanged.
- No REST or MCP change.

## Files in scope

- `apps/web/src/routes/insights/**` (the summary line, `insights-data.ts`)

## Report (filled by engineer)

Files changed: `packages/core/src/period-summary.ts` (`summarisePeriods` averages every period
given whose change is not null; `avgPct` is the mean change over `|first.endValue −
first.change|`, worked from the exact BigInt sum, and null when that opening total is 0; `avgAbs`
unchanged, half away from zero) and `period-summary.test.ts`. Web: the summary line moves out of
`routes/insights/Insights.tsx` into a new `Summary.tsx` with `Summary.module.css` (its four rules
leave `Insights.module.css`), typed by core's `PeriodSummary`, and states the amount alone when
`avgPct` is null. `Insights.tsx` drops `fullSummary` and `rangedTooSmall`: since a range always
holds the newest period, it has an average whenever the full set does, so the chart placeholder
reads "Need more data" when there is no average and "Pick a longer range" otherwise (only the
assets-vs-liabilities chart, which needs two period ends, can then be empty). Tests: new
`Summary.test.tsx`, `insights-data.test.ts`, `e2e/insights.spec.ts`.

Tests run / results: `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck` green. `pnpm test`: 135 files / 1518 tests
pass. The core tests were written first and failed (11 of 12) before the change: the report's
months read −$140 and −1.6% (count 5), with and without the series' first period leading; a
ranged view's oldest period counts; a negative opening total measures against its size (paying
down a debt reads as a gain); an opening of 0 gives a null percent; the percent comes from the
exact sum, not the rounded mean; the engine's periods in all three views, the percent derived
independently from the first and last end values. The property test (fast-check, as in
`periods.test.ts`) runs series that rise, fall and cross zero, with and without a leading first
period, and checks the count, the rounding, the percent's sign against the exact mean and never
against the amount, and the percent's value; six named series pin rising, falling and crossing
zero either way, two of which the old mean of percents got wrong. `period-summary.ts` has 100%
line coverage (core: 99.85%). The web summary tests failed first too (2 of 4): the report's months
read "Averaging +$825 (+13.9%) a month over the last 4 months" as a range, and an opening of 0
read "(0%)". `pnpm test:int` (Postgres): 58 files / 737 tests pass. E2E (`e2e/insights.spec.ts`):
32 passed at 390 and 1280 px, including the report's months as a fictional ledger ("Averaging
−$140 (−1.6%) a month over the last 5 months."), an opening of 0 ("Averaging +$500 a month over
the last 2 months."), the full monthly view (+240% over 12 months) and 6M, which now counts all
six months.

Deviations: `Summary.tsx`, `Summary.module.css` and `Summary.test.tsx` are new files under
`routes/insights/`, so the line can be rendered and tested alone; the move is its own commit with
no change in wording. A one-period range (YTD in January, or quarterly YTD in the first quarter)
now shows its average, the growth bar and the distribution column, where it showed placeholders
and "needs two months of values"; it follows from counting a range's oldest period and matches
the prototype. It depends on the run date, so no E2E pins it. The prototype's `renderInsights`
still averages the periods' percents; it is outside the files in scope, and `decisions.md` holds
the rule.

Open issues: none for this task. The percent has the exact mean's sign, while the amount is
rounded to whole minor units, so a mean under half a minor unit reads $0; its percent can only
round to a nonzero figure when the opening total is under 500 minor units.
