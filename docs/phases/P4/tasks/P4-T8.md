# P4-T8: The privacy policy and terms — drafts for legal review, and their pages

Status: done
Depends on: P4-T2 (ordering: the pages are built with the landing page)
Parallel-safe: yes

## Goal

A privacy policy and terms of use, drafted from what NetNet actually does, ready for the Founder's
legal review (F23), and served at `/privacy` and `/terms`. The directory listings (P4-T7) and
Stage 8's legal basics need both.

## Context

- PRD §2.6 and A7: P-B and P-C ask where the data lives, whether it is sold and what deletion
  means before signing up, so the policy is a go-to-market document, not boilerplate. It must say
  what is true, plainly, and nothing more.
- `decisions.md` (Delivery): P4 drafts both from the architecture's facts; the Founder has them
  reviewed by someone qualified (F23) before they are published as final or used in a listing.
  The drafts say "Draft" at the top until the Founder records the review.
- The facts to draw on, each with its source:
  - What is collected: the account (email, password hash or provider link), profile (country, time
    zone, segment), the balance sheet the person or their agent enters, connections to agents
    (client name, times), push endpoints, reminder state; analytics events with no amounts, names
    or text (architecture §6, §7.7; PRD §3.5); logs and error reports scrubbed of content (§10).
  - What is not: documents and statements never reach NetNet (N2); no tracking or advertising SDKs;
    no data sold or shared for marketing.
  - Where: Neon Postgres and the function in Singapore, files on Vercel's CDN; processors Vercel,
    Neon, Resend (email), Sentry (errors), the browsers' push services, Google and Apple for sign-in
    (§4, §5, §7.10). Encryption at rest and in transit (§8).
  - The person's agent: what a connection may do, that every change is logged, how to revoke it,
    the security email on every connection (J5, J7).
  - Retention and deletion: export any time (J10); deletion erases at once and backups expire
    within 30 days, the stated window (§8); events re-keyed to a random id (§6.4); the comeback
    reminder and how to turn it off (J12).
  - Cookies: one first-party session cookie; the landing page sets none (§6.4, P4-T2).
  - Who runs it, and a contact address (the Founder supplies both).
  - Terms: free, what stays free (§4.2); NetNet states facts and never gives advice, and the
    person's own agent is theirs (§1, §2.4, N2); acceptable use; the service as is; changes to the
    terms.
- Singapore's PDPA is the starting point (the operator and data are in Singapore); note in the
  draft where other launch markets' rules may need the reviewer's eye. The draft is not legal
  advice and says so to the Founder in the PR, not on the page.

## Report (filled by engineer)
Files changed:
- `apps/landing/privacy.html`, `apps/landing/terms.html` (new): the two drafts, built from the
  landing page's own header, footer, fonts and tokens. Each opens with a visible `Draft` note
  saying it is not final and waiting for a qualified review, names no reviewer or date, and says
  so. The privacy page covers what is held, what never reaches NetNet, the processors and where
  the data is, the agent connection, the one session cookie and the cookie-less landing visit,
  reminders, export and deletion, and security. The terms page covers what NetNet is and is not,
  the account, what stays free, the person's data, the agent's access, acceptable use, the service
  as it is, changes and ending it. Both end with the questions the reviewer should settle,
  starting from Singapore's PDPA. Neither page loads `src/main.ts`, so neither sends an event or
  sets a cookie.
- `apps/landing/vite.config.ts`: `index.html`, `privacy.html` and `terms.html` are the three Vite
  inputs, so the build writes `dist/privacy.html` and `dist/terms.html` beside `dist/index.html`.

  44px `.quiet` target.
- `apps/landing/src/styles.css`: the document column (centred, 44em), its headings and prose, the
  draft note on `--warn-bg`, and the footer's flex row. Tokens only; no new motion.
- `vercel.json`: `/privacy` → `/privacy.html` and `/terms` → `/terms.html` rewrites, and the two
  routes join the pages' no-cache header source.
- `test/headers/headers.test.ts`: the page-cache source now covers the legal routes, and a new
  case asserts both rewrites exist and the global security headers still apply.
